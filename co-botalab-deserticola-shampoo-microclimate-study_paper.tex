\begin{abstract}
Background: The Colombian market presents unique dermatological challenges due to diverse microclimates ranging from high-humidity Caribbean coasts to cool, high-altitude Andean zones. This study evaluates the performance of BOTALAB's Deserticola Plus Shampoo, formulated with patented hair amino acids, minerals, Wild Olive Stem Cells, and Chlorella Ferment. Methods: A stratified consumer survey (N=200) was conducted across Bogotá, Medellín, and Cartagena, assessing Likert-scale perceptions on foam texture, post-rinse dryness, fragrance stability, and barrier retention duration. Quantitative Results: The composite mean score was 3.61/5.00, with a Top-2-Box percentage of 58.4\%. Notably, 71.5\% of respondents reported hydration effects lasting 12–24 hours. Vegan amino-acid surfactants received the highest approval (mean 4.0, Top-2-Box 84.5\%), while patent-specific claims regarding Wild Olive Stem Cells showed lower trust (mean 3.38). Strategic Implications: While product efficacy is validated, distribution limitations (cited by 100\% of non-buyers) and unfamiliarity with patents remain critical barriers. Regulatory alignment with INVIMA standards is perceived as adequate but requires stronger third-party validation to enhance Net Promoter Score (currently -0.5).

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} BOTALAB, Deserticola Plus, Microclimate Adaptation, Colombia Skincare, INVIMA Compliance, Hair Barrier Retention
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The Deserticola Plus Shampoo formulation leverages four core patented technologies: Deserticola Plus (hair amino acids + 4 minerals), Wild Olive Stem Cell, Chlorella Ferment, and Vegan amino-acid surfactants. The study methodology involved a stratified random sampling of 200 female consumers in Colombia, segmented by city microclimate (Cartagena: 81\% RH; Medellín: 68\% RH; Bogotá: 72\% RH). Participants evaluated specific sensory and functional metrics using a 5-point Likert scale. The primary objective was to determine if the formulation's '12-Hour Barrier Retention' claim holds true under varying humidity conditions. The sample included diverse occupational backgrounds, from C-End corporate managers to B2B aesthetic clinic owners, ensuring a holistic view of both personal use and professional recommendation potential.
\section{2. Quantitative Metrics \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative analysis reveals strong performance in sensory attributes. Foam texture lightweight perception scored a mean of 3.92 (Top-2-Box 83.5\%), indicating suitability for high-humidity environments where residue buildup is a common concern. Post-rinse hair dryness minimalism achieved a mean of 3.87 (Top-2-Box 79.0\%). Crucially, 71.5\% of users confirmed that hydration effects last between 12 to 24 hours, directly supporting the core marketing claim. However, fragrance profile stability in high ambient humidity received a moderate score of 3.65 (Top-2-Box 64.0\%), suggesting room for improvement in scent longevity. The vegan amino-acid surfactant system was the most praised feature (mean 4.0, Top-2-Box 84.5\%), highlighting consumer preference for sulfate-free daily cleansers over traditional options.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Demographic segmentation shows nuanced preferences. Affluent/High Net Worth individuals reported higher satisfaction (mean 3.81) compared to Lower-Middle Income groups (mean 3.48), likely driven by price sensitivity (mean 3.60). In terms of personality traits, INTPs and ISFPs exhibited distinct behaviors; INTPs focused heavily on ingredient purity and mechanism verification, while ISFPs prioritized texture and aesthetic finish. B2B stakeholders, such as aesthetic clinic owners, valued 'Barrier repair speed' (90\% selection rate) and 'Ingredient purity' (96\%) as key adoption drivers. Conversely, C-End users were more influenced by brand trust (75.5\%). Detractors in the NPS calculation (12\%) primarily cited 'Limited distribution channels' (100\% selection rate among barriers) and 'Unfamiliarity with patents' (83.5\%), indicating a gap in educational marketing.
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Strategy}
Compliance with INVIMA regulations is perceived positively (mean 3.50, Top-2-Box 49.5\%), though not overwhelmingly so. To improve confidence in the '12-hour barrier retention' claim, 95\% of respondents requested Dermatologist endorsements, and 92.5\% sought Third-party lab tests. The current Net Promoter Score of -0.5 reflects a passive majority (76.5\%) who are satisfied but not enthusiastic advocates. Strategic recommendations include expanding digital distribution to mitigate channel limitations and launching targeted educational campaigns explaining the specific benefits of Wild Olive Stem Cells and Chlorella Ferment. Aligning marketing materials with visible before-after photos (requested by 52.5\%) could further bridge the trust gap for skeptical segments.

\section{Conclusion}
The RIMAN Research study confirms that BOTALAB Deserticola Plus Shampoo delivers robust microclimate adaptation and barrier retention in Colombian markets, particularly through its vegan amino-acid surfactant technology. While sensory metrics like foam lightness and post-rinse moisture are highly rated, the low familiarity with proprietary patents and limited distribution channels hinder broader adoption. Enhancing third-party clinical validation and expanding accessible retail networks are critical steps to convert passive users into promoters and solidify the brand's position against dermatological pharmacy competitors.
